The Complete Guide to Amber Fort:
Rajasthan's Crown Jewel

📅 June 2025 · ⏱ 8 min read · 👁 4.2k views · ✍️ VacationPe Editorial
Home › Rajasthan › Amber Fort

Rising from the Aravalli hills like a golden mirage, Amber Fort is more than a monument — it is Rajasthan's soul set in stone. Explore every hidden chamber, secret passage, and breathtaking view that this 16th-century masterpiece has to offer before you visit.

Perched 11 kilometres from Jaipur on a rocky hill overlooking Maota Lake, Amber Fort (also spelled Amer Fort) is arguably the finest example of Rajput military architecture in all of India. Built from pale yellow and pink sandstone, the fort complex took nearly 100 years to complete — begun by Raja Man Singh I in 1592 and expanded by successive Kachwaha rulers. Today, it is one of Rajasthan's six hill forts listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site (2013).

A Brief History of Amber Fort

The Kachwaha clan of Rajputs chose this strategic hilltop to guard the ancient trade route between Delhi and Gujarat. What began as a modest defensive structure evolved, generation by generation, into a sprawling royal residence of extraordinary beauty. Raja Man Singh I — a general in Emperor Akbar's army — used the wealth from Mughal patronage to build the fort's foundations. His successors, Raja Jai Singh I and Mirza Raja Jai Singh, added the palaces and ornamental courtyards that define its character today.

Every stone of Amber tells a story — of Rajput pride, of Mughal friendship, of queens who watched the world from latticed windows.

What to See Inside the Fort

Diwan-i-Aam — The Hall of Public Audience

The first grand courtyard you enter holds the Diwan-i-Aam, where the maharaja would hold public court. Forty columns support a double-storey arcade, and the raised royal throne at the far end still commands the space with authority. Note the intricate jali (lattice) screens on the upper galleries — these allowed royal women to observe proceedings without being seen.

Ganesh Pol — The Most Photographed Gateway

Built by Mirza Raja Jai Singh around 1640, the Ganesh Pol is the private entrance to the royal apartments. Its facade is a riot of colour — mosaic tiles, fresco paintings, and a large image of Lord Ganesh above the archway. Every inch is decorated; it is considered one of the most ornate gateways in Indian architecture.

Sheesh Mahal — The Palace of Mirrors

This is Amber Fort's crown jewel. The Sheesh Mahal (Palace of Mirrors) gets its name from thousands of convex mirror pieces set into the ceiling and walls in intricate floral and geometric patterns. When a single candle is lit inside, it reflects as a field of stars. The effect is utterly magical — and unlike anything else you will see in India.

📌 Insider Tip Ask your guide to light a match inside the Sheesh Mahal. Watch the entire ceiling explode into a galaxy of reflected light. It takes your breath away every single time.

Jai Mandir & Sukh Niwas

Adjacent to the Sheesh Mahal, the Jai Mandir is a formal reception room with glass inlay work and marble relief panels. Just opposite, the Sukh Niwas (Hall of Pleasure) was an ingeniously designed cooling chamber — water flowed through channels carved into the marble walls to lower the temperature by several degrees. Rajput air conditioning, effectively.

The Zenana — Royal Women's Quarters

The maharaja's 12 wives each had a separate apartment in the Zenana, arranged around a shared courtyard but cleverly designed so no wife could see into another's chambers. Each apartment had its own entrance from the maharaja's corridor — so he could visit without the others knowing. The ingenuity (and perhaps the diplomacy) required is impressive.

Amber Fort Ramparts & Views

Walk the outer ramparts for sweeping views of Maota Lake, the Aravalli hills, and the serpentine wall connecting Amber to Jaigarh Fort on the ridge above. The walk takes about 20 minutes and rewards you with some of the best landscape photography opportunities in Rajasthan.

How to Reach Amber Fort from Jaipur

  • By Auto/Cab: Most convenient — ₹200–350 one way from Jaipur city centre. Book through your hotel or Ola/Uber.
  • By Bus (JCTSL): City bus Route 5 from Badi Chaupar. Cheapest option at ₹30, but slower.
  • By Jeep Shared: Jeeps run from Hawa Mahal area to Amber — ₹50–70 per person.
  • On Foot (up to fort): From the base, a well-maintained ramp leads up to the fort entrance — takes about 15 minutes walking.
🐘 On the Elephant Ride The elephant ride up to Ganesh Pol is iconic but controversial. Animal welfare organisations have raised concerns about the elephants' conditions. Consider the jeep or walking route as a gentler alternative — and the views are equally spectacular.

Best Time to Visit Amber Fort

Amber Fort can be visited year-round, but conditions vary significantly by season:

  • October – February: Perfect weather, 15–25°C, golden morning light ideal for photography. Busiest season.
  • March – April: Warming up but still pleasant. Crowds thin slightly after mid-March.
  • May – June: Very hot (40°C+). Visit at opening time (8 AM) or late afternoon only.
  • July – September: Monsoon. The fort and surrounding hills turn dramatically green. Beautiful for photography, fewer crowds.

Combine With: Jaigarh Fort & Nahargarh

Amber Fort is best explored as part of a Jaipur fort triangle. A short walk (or jeep ride) up the hill connects Amber to Jaigarh Fort, which houses the world's largest cannon on wheels — Jaivana — and a fascinating weapons museum. Add Nahargarh Fort at sunset for the most spectacular view over pink Jaipur.

Photography Tips at Amber Fort

  • Arrive at 8 AM sharp for the first hour of golden light with almost no other tourists
  • The lakeside reflection shot is taken from the road below — stop before entering the fort complex
  • The Ganesh Pol courtyard at midday creates beautiful shadow patterns through the jali screens
  • For the Sheesh Mahal interior, bring a wide-angle lens or use Portrait mode on your phone
  • The rampart walk at 4–5 PM catches the hills in warm amber light
